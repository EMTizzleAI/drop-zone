🚨 Immediate danger?

If someone is seriously hurt, a life is in immediate danger, or you cannot stay safe right now, call emergency services. In the U.S., call 911. Tell the dispatcher your location and what's happening as clearly and honestly as you can.

🩷 What happens when you ask for help?

Medical professionals, therapists, school personnel, and some other professionals may have reporting or emergency-response duties. The rules depend on your location, role, age, and circumstances. Speaking to a professional does not automatically mean you'll be hospitalized or placed on an involuntary hold.

They may ask about immediate safety, symptoms, and what support you need. If you want a psychiatric evaluation, you can ask for one directly. An evaluation is not the same thing as automatic hospitalization.

Be truthful about danger, symptoms, and whether you can stay safe. You can ask what is confidential, what might require reporting, and what voluntary options are available before sharing details. Don't minimize immediate danger just to avoid an evaluation.

“I'm struggling and I'd like help understanding my options. Can you explain confidentiality and when you would need to involve emergency services? I'd like to know about voluntary evaluation and outpatient care.”

🤝 What if I don't have insurance?

Start with your county behavioral health access line, a community health center, or 211. In California, Medi-Cal covers eligible behavioral health services; county mental health plans coordinate specialty care. Some community programs also serve uninsured people. Availability and cost vary.
